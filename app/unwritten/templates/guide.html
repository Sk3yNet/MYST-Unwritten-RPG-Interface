The Game Master’s Guide

For first-time GMs: how to run an Unwritten session with this app, in about ten minutes of reading. The rulebook goes deeper; this gets you to the table.

Your job, in one paragraph

You describe the world, play everyone the explorers meet, and decide what happens when they act. You are not the players’ opponent: you are the author of the obstacles and the biggest fan of the explorers. Your three questions, over and over: “What do you do?”, “How do you do it?” and “Do we need to roll for that?”

Before the first session

  1. Read the adventure once (GM Screen ▸ load it on the Desk). You don’t need to memorize it: every scene’s notes are on screen when you need them.
  2. Send the invite link from the Desk. Players sign in with Google and land on their own screen.
  3. Make characters together (session zero, or the first 30–45 minutes). Quick start is enough: a name, a high concept, and a Great (+4) skill. Ask each player one question about their explorer’s past and suggest it as an aspect.
  4. Talk about the table: what kind of story everyone wants, and anything anyone would rather not have in the game.

Running a session with this app

  1. Start the session on The Table ▸ GM: scene & pacing ▸ New session (fate points refill to refresh).
  2. Start the scene on the GM Screen: pick it and press Start scene. The scene board fills with its aspects, players see its map, and its first areas are revealed.
  3. Read the boxed text aloud, then ask “What do you do?”
  4. Lift the fog as they move: click an area ▸ Reveal. Or let players ask: their requests appear on your screen, and you can reveal, call for a roll (a success reveals it automatically), or say “not yet.”
  5. Show things. When they find a journal page or link to a new Age, push the handout from the area panel or the Handouts list. It fills their screens.
  6. Answer Discover questions from the area’s Secrets. Each question earned deserves a true, useful answer.
  7. Offer compels from the Compel ideas panel: one or two per player per session keeps fate points flowing.
  8. End the scene when its question is answered (the notes say when). At the end of the session, award a minor milestone.

When to roll, and how hard

Roll only when failing would be interesting and the outcome is uncertain. Opening an unlocked door: no roll. Opening it while the water rises: roll.

DifficultyMeans
Mediocre (+0)Anyone could, but there’s some pressure
Adequate (+1)Easy for a trained explorer
Fair (+2)Routine for a professional: your default when unsure
Good (+3)Hard; most people fail without help
Great (+4)Very hard; plan, prepare, invoke aspects
Superb (+5) and upThe stuff of stories told afterward

Pick the action by what they want: Overcome to get past something, Create an advantage to set something up (a new aspect with free invokes), Discover to learn things (one question per shift), Oppose when someone resists.

When a roll fails

Failure should change the situation, not stop the story. On a tie or fumble, the player can usually choose to succeed at a cost. Good costs:

  • Time: “You get it open, but the water is at your waist now.”
  • A complication: “It opens, and something in the dark heard it.”
  • A consequence: “You squeeze through, and take Scraped Raw as a minor consequence.”
  • An advantage for the other side: an NPC gets a boost or a free invoke.

If you can’t think of a cost, ask the table: players are full of terrible ideas for their own characters.

Fate points, invokes and compels

  • Invoke: after rolling, a player spends a fate point on an aspect that fits for +2 or a reroll. Let them, as long as they say how it helps.
  • Compel: you suggest an aspect makes things harder (“You Can’t Resist a Locked Door… so you open it before anyone’s ready?”). Accept: they gain a fate point. Refuse: they pay one. Compels are how players earn points, so offer them.
  • Watch the coins on the Table: if someone’s out of fate points, look for a compel.

When things go sideways

The players are stuck
Use the scene’s “If they’re stuck” notes, or offer a Discover roll, or a deduction (Deduction tab) to reason it out together. A hint is never a failure.
They do something the adventure didn’t plan for
Great! Ask yourself what would really happen, say yes if you can, and let the notes catch up later. You can reveal any area or show any handout at any time.
You don’t know a rule
Make a call, keep playing, look it up afterward (Rules tab). Consistency matters more than precision.
One player dominates
Turn to someone else: “While that’s happening, what are you doing?”
